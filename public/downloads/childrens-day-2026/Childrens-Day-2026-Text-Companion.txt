A simple opening
Children have rights because they are children. Rights are not prizes for good behaviour.
Governments and adults have responsibilities to uphold them. We can respect one
another while asking adults to remove barriers. Today we will explore what helps every
child belong and be heard.

Make participation safe and accessible
Offer drawing, writing, speaking, home languages, assistive communication, an adult scribe or a quiet

Use fictional situations. Do not ask a child to represent a culture, race, disability or family. Discuss

The outdoor learning plan
A school wants to improve its outdoor learning area. Adults invite ideas through one lunchtime
meeting. Students must speak to the whole group. The plan is chosen before children who
missed the meeting can respond.
Rights lens | selected UNCRC articles, paraphrased
2: Rights apply without discrimination. 12: Children can express views about matters affecting them, and
adults take those views seriously. 23: Children with disabilities must be able to participate in community
life.
1. Evidence: What does the scenario actually tell us?
2. A question, not a guess: What do we still need to find out?
3. Claim + right: Which article helps explain a barrier to being heard?
4. Proposal: What should adults change? Give two ways to contribute.
5. Check: What evidence would show that participation improved?
Think further: Fairness is not always the same support for everyone. Whose ideas might still be missing?
No student has to speak for a whole group. Adults explain the final decision and how children's views
influenced it.

Before inviting ideas
Ask an adult sponsor to agree to a manageable scope, private collection method, who reads
responses and when students will hear back. If this is not ready, practise with fictional ideas instead.
1 Invite, without pressure
Ask: What helps every child belong and be heard? Invite an optional anonymous drawing or note,
or a conversation with an adult. Offer accessible ways and enough time. A quiet pass is welcome.
OPTIONAL IDEA SLIP | No name needed
One thing that could help more children belong is...
2 Notice patterns, not people
With the adult, group ideas without names or identifying details. Notice quieter or less common
suggestions as well as popular ones. A majority vote cannot take away another child's rights.
3 Choose one achievable proposal
Check it against three criteria: removes a barrier; includes more ways to participate; has adult support
and a realistic next step. Ask who may still be left out before choosing.
